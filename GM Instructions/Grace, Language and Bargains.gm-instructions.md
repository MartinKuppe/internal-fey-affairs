# Grace, Language and Bargains

This dossier explains the culture behind the mechanics. The **Grace**, **Bargains & Contracts**, and **Reputation Markers** sections of **System Rules** remain mechanically authoritative whenever shorthand here could be read more than one way.

## Grace as social currency

Grace lies between money, gratitude, obligation, and courtesy. A queen or court grants its own currency, often in return for service. Subjects and outsiders then use it to exchange goods, services, favours, apologies, and recognition.

The ordinary verbal pattern is:

- **Please** and similar pleading language propose an implied bargain. Stronger pleading signals a greater amount the speaker is prepared to pay. The word alone transfers nothing; a rejected plea costs nothing.
- **Thank you** and similar gratitude settle an accepted and performed plea. Under System Rules, the amount transferred is the greater of the amount implied by the plea and the thanks. Thanks may also be agreed in advance.
- Further gratitude after thanks—“much obliged,” “you have been very helpful,” and similar wording—may constitute a tip when the context clearly indicates an additional transfer. Record any real transfer in the Grace Ledger rather than treating every polite flourish as automatic.
- **You’re welcome** and similar replies acknowledge receipt. They do not normally transfer Grace.
- **Sorry** and similar apologetic language may offer Grace as compensation for a real or perceived wrong. An apology can repair the social breach while a ledger transfer settles any separate debt; do not assume that an isolated word silently creates an amount.
- **It’s okay** and similar replies acknowledge or forgive an apology. They do not normally transfer Grace.

Always identify the relevant queen-issued currency from affiliation and context. Queen’s Graces are the general Royal currency. Other court currencies apply when both parties belong, or are sufficiently affiliated, to that court. The authoritative currency records live in the **Grace** folder.

## Truth and deception

Actual Fey are physically incapable of knowingly making a false assertion. Attempting it is comparable to deliberately tearing out one’s own eye. Their language is precise, and they experience a close coupling between a statement and the reality it describes.

This does not make them candid. A Fey may deliberately deceive by:

- answering the question asked rather than the question intended;
- emphasising selected true facts;
- omitting crucial information;
- exploiting double meanings;
- using technically accurate language that invites a wrong interpretation;
- speaking a metaphor that is literally true but will be taken figuratively;
- asking a question instead of asserting anything;
- choosing an atypical example the listener will wrongly generalise; or
- remaining silent, laughing, or making a gesture the listener will misread.

When writing Fey dialogue, verify the literal proposition. “Misdirecting” is not a licence to insert a knowingly false assertion.

This limitation belongs to actual Fey. Human Takelings and human-derived clones can lie. Morrigan can lie because she is secretly the human Rioghnach; do not reveal that origin merely to explain an apparent lie.

## Bargains

A Fey regards failing an accepted bargain as almost as unthinkable and ugly as lying. Bargains bind the letter, not the presumed spirit, and elegant literal interpretation is culturally admired. Exact wording therefore matters and belongs in the Bargain file.

Humans remain physically capable of breach. Breaking a bargain with a Fey brings severe social and practical consequences—at minimum the crossed Fey’s ire—and may create an appropriate Reputation Marker or further settlement. Never invent a loophole after the fact: it must follow from the recorded wording and circumstances.
