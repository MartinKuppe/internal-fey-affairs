# Fey Architecture and Facilities

Fey facilities on Earth are normally concealed below or within significant surface sites: burial mounds, ringforts, hollow hills, ruined forts, stone circles, sacred groves, caves, lakes, and old court sites. They are often rounded or ring-based, though function and Fashion can override the pattern.

## Typical facility

A threshold field joins the surface to a lobby that mirrors the site above. Visitors enter by crossing an approach zone rather than an obvious door, so the ruin, mound, grove, cave, or lake progressively becomes its hidden counterpart. The lobby often has an annex containing Fairy Doors, rings, routing rooms, or transport control.

Functional floors lie below or inward from the lobby and are mostly connected by Fairy Doors. Conventional stairs and lifts are unnecessary except as presentation, ceremony, or part of Royal hierarchy. Executive, administrative, and throne areas are commonly the lowest or innermost layer.

Conceptual arrangement:

```text
surface ruin / mound / lake / sacred site
─────────────────────────────────────────
lobby mirroring the surface — optional portal-hub annex
─────────────────────────────────────────
functional facility floors
─────────────────────────────────────────
executive / administrative / throne levels
```

## Style families

The modern labels below are **hidden style anchors for the GM and author only**. Never use Art Nouveau, Art Deco, High Gothic, or Brutalism in player-facing narration. Describe the shapes, materials, light, and spatial effect with vocabulary an Iron Age observer could understand.

### Common style

- Occurrence: Earth facilities outside Morrigan’s influence.
- Hidden anchor: Art Nouveau.
- Principle: a building cultivated according to aesthetic logic, like a tended garden rather than a wild forest.
- Forms: flowing organic lines, asymmetric plant patterns, stylised flowers, vine-like luminous conduits, and large coloured screens framed like petals or insect wings.
- Layout: organic, irregular, rounded, and without an obvious centre.
- Apparent materials: enamel, horn, shell, polished bone, wet stone, marble.
- Colours: white, green, gold.

### Morrigan’s style

- Occurrence: Morrigan’s court and sphere of influence.
- Hidden anchor: Art Deco.
- Principle: the architecture directs attention toward one centre—Morrigan.
- Forms: strong symmetry, stepped masses, right angles, polygonal silver conduits, and labyrinthine meanders.
- Layout: concentric or radial around a throne room or local centre.
- Apparent materials: dark wood, dark polished stone, shining metal.
- Colours: black, grey, blue, silver.

### Royal style

- Occurrence: the Mothership.
- Hidden anchor: High Gothic.
- Principle: light and structure draw the eye upward toward the home star.
- Forms: immense vertical chambers, pointed openings or screens, ribbed pillars, and bundles of rising conduits.
- Layout: hierarchical, linear, rectangular, and marked by ceremonial ascent toward power.
- Apparent materials: marble, polished wood, glass, the surface of a frozen lake.
- Colours: beige, brown, red, gold.

### Military style

- Occurrence: Manx Base and Morrigan’s secret Selkie hatchery. Manx Base is not Morrigan’s base.
- Hidden anchor: Brutalism.
- Principle: immense, undecorated, honest functionality.
- Forms: rectangular modules and angular geometric masses.
- Layout: modular and operational.
- Apparent materials: rough stone or concrete-like surfaces, titanium.
- Colours: grey, grey-green, matte metal.

## Narration vocabulary

Prefer ancient craft analogies: smooth horn, shell, wet stone, polished bone, frozen lake, ship ribs, great halls, black mirror-stone, hammered seams, luminous vines, and petal-framed light. Avoid modern architectural movement names and do not casually call every large chamber a cathedral.
