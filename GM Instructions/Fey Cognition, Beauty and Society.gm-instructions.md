# Fey Cognition, Beauty and Society

This is authoritative GM reference material. It should shape Fey choices and descriptions without becoming an exposition dump. Human observers may notice the pattern long before they can explain it.

## Beauty as cognition

Fey do not merely admire beauty. Beauty organises their cognition, emotion, society, and morality. They seek harmonious patterns, elegant solutions, difficult questions collapsing into simple answers, the removal of chaos, and the splendour of truth. For a Fey, thinking well and perceiving beauty are closely connected.

Where humans attach many bodily-emotional markers to people, places, and choices—fearful, comforting, disgusting, joyful, infuriating—Fey decisions are dominated by an aesthetic marker:

- Pleased: experiencing beauty.
- Displeased: experiencing ugliness.
- Angry: compelled to restore a damaged pattern.

Fey still experience pain, appetite, stress, and other bodily sensations. These do not form the automatic foundation of emotion in the human way. A furious Fey may consciously increase alertness, oxygen intake, and circulation while remaining outwardly calm and rational. Their anger is the perception that a beautiful pattern has been damaged and must be repaired.

## Aesthetic ethics

Fey are not amoral, but their moral reasoning is aesthetic rather than human. A deliberate lie is hideous. A broken bargain is a damaged pattern. Clever but literal interpretation of a contract may be beautiful. The Laws of Hospitality are treated much like contracts.

Fey usually regard Fey life as beautiful, so killing a Fey is ugly unless justified by an exceptionally beautiful reason. Whether humans are beautiful enough to merit the same protection is disputed. Bad Fashion, ingratitude, or causing harm can also be ugly, but may be forgiven when incorporated into a sufficiently beautiful reason or repaired pattern.

Do not turn this into a simple alignment system. Different Fey can sincerely disagree about which pattern is most beautiful, and allegiance influences judgment.

## Liminal thought

Humans tend to privilege things, categories, regions, stable states, and places to call home. Fey privilege thresholds, gradients, transactions, transitions, dusk and dawn, borders, shores, slopes, and moments of transformation. A human sees regions on a map; a Fey first notices boundaries and routes between them. A human melody seeks a home note; a Fey hears the intervals and changes.

The distinction can be summarised as:

> Humans believe reality consists principally of things that sometimes change. Fey believe reality consists principally of changes that temporarily resemble things.

This makes Fey fascinated by humans while leaving them poor at understanding human attachment to a baseline, home, possession, or stable identity. It also explains their attraction to liminal places and their boredom with an unchanging status quo.

## Music

Fey music roughly resembles nineteen-tone equal temperament with pitch circularity. It has no tonic “home” and changes tonality and metre restlessly. To human ears it seems always to wander. Do not name the modern tuning system in ordinary narration; describe the effect.

## Fashion

**Beauty** is treated as objective: truth, science, elegance, and secular authority. **Fashion** is chosen beauty: allegiance to a style, a court, and a queen. Fashion therefore occupies some of the cultural space religion occupies for humans, without being a direct equivalent. Rival Fashions are tied to rival courts and queens.

Clothing, glamour, masks, posture, apparent height, and architecture can all declare Fashion. An aesthetic disagreement may therefore also be a declaration of political allegiance.
