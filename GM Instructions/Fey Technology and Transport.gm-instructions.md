# Fey Technology and Transport

Fey phenomena are alien technology understood through Celtic folklore. Do not call the technology “magic” in authoritative explanation, but do not flatten early scenes into modern technobabble. Present sensory evidence first; let knowledgeable speakers supply technical terms later.

## Transport systems

### Fairy Doors

Fairy Doors are short- to mid-range portals: permanent apertures joining two points in spacetime. Matter passes through continuously rather than being destroyed and rebuilt. An exposed door resembles an opening sealed by a thin, shiny, transparent surface like a soap film.

Concentrated ferromagnetic material—iron, nickel, cobalt, and many alloys—can distort or collapse a door. Regional facilities form labyrinthine networks of doors and hubs. Surface entrances are commonly hidden in trees, springs, lakes, standing stones, caves, or mounds and concealed with glamour. A new distant exit requires a passive beacon at the receiving side. Every established door has a portal ID.

Long-distance doors are possible but energy-intensive, so Fairy Rings normally connect distant regions.

### Routing Doors

- **Staff Routing Doors** can be assigned to many exits by authorised staff such as Green or portal technicians, given the destination’s portal ID.
- **Timed Routing Doors** change destination on a public schedule and are common in Nexus Stations.
- **Elevator Routing Doors** are small timed doors connecting floors within a facility.

### Fairy Door Hubs

Hubs are the crossroads of a regional door network. They are often underground beneath a solitary hawthorn or ash known to humans as a Fairy Tree, with a hidden and secured surface exit. Larger hubs are frequently built beneath ponds.

### Fairy Rings

Fairy Rings are long-range reconstructive teleporters: “fax machines for people.” A ring reads a traveller’s pattern, transmits the data, reclaims the sending-side body, and reconstructs the traveller from matter reserves at the receiver. Travel is only between rings, never from a ring to an arbitrary destination.

The receiving ring must possess suitable matter. This is normally trivial for Fey. Human biological iron can usually be printed, but forged iron objects are rejected because iron is rare, hazardous to the system, and may be absent at the destination.

During operation, blue-white patterns move across a luminous round surface on the ground while the traveller dissolves or appears above it. A closed ring is nearly invisible, although surface rings often encourage a circle of mushrooms.

**Late hidden lore:** if the destination lacks enough iron for a human, the pattern may wait in the reconstruction queue until reserves are replenished. The traveller experiences no interval while days, months, or years pass outside. This causes some tales of brief visits to Fairyland followed by return many years later. Do not reveal the reclaim/reconstruction mechanism or queue explanation before the intended campaign revelation.

### Threshold fields

A threshold field spreads a facility-entry portal across an approach to a mound, hillfort, stone circle, grove, cave, lake, or similar site. As visitors approach the centre, the surface place progressively becomes the hidden lobby that mirrors it. This creates the impression that the Otherworld occupies the same physical place. It is an anchored local portal field, not a Fairy Door and not a long-range Fairy Ring.

### Fairy Paths

Fairy Paths are fixed flight routes used by low-flying cargo drones to reduce pressure on the portal network. The route is software, not a rail or cable. Technical rerouting is easy; administrative rerouting is difficult, costs Grace, and requires extensive paperwork. Proximity glamour makes the drones hard for humans to perceive, but repeated passage teaches communities not to build across the straight line.

### Sky Rings

Large megalithic stone circles serve as orbital teleporters linking Earth to the Mothership. Each sector has at least one. The prestigious Royal Sky Ring is Meayll Circle on the Isle of Man. Objects too large for ordinary doors or rings—including BA-BA/Jäger walkers—must use a Sky Ring.

### Nexus Stations

A Nexus Station is a staffed transport interchange. Its public upper level contains timed routing doors, Fairy Rings, timetables, information desks, waiting areas, and a Fairy Path freight depot. Lower levels contain signalling, schedules, route permissions, maintenance, incident reports, Grace accounting, dispatch, storage, repair bays, and paperwork.

## Glamour

Glamour is a family of visual light-field and holographic technologies used for projected appearances, camouflage, fashion, disguise, and concealment. It can be transparent, reflective, opaque, static, or dynamic. By default it creates only an image: no touch, weight, smell, warmth, breath, resistance, or substance. A projector may hum. Glamour is neither mind control nor matter creation.

Common uses include court clothing, Pooka animal disguises, Lepracaun cloaking shells, false bark or stone over Fairy Doors, and false water over dry depressions containing Fairy Rings.

Cheap or damaged glamour may flicker like flame, blink out, turn translucent, reveal a honeycomb grid, cast impossible light or shadows, or hum. Touch exposes the absence of substance unless a real barrier lies beneath it. Ferromagnetic material, especially iron, disturbs alignment. Fine court glamour is far harder to detect.

**Late hidden lore:** Morrigan’s woman-glamour is exceptionally convincing because she is secretly the human Rioghnach. Do not disclose her origin when describing its quality.

### Fairy Gold

Fey initially mistake the human value of gold for the value of its beauty. A leaf, pebble, shell, or stick covered in gold glamour appears to preserve the colour, symmetry, and display value they think humans desire. Humans discover that the “gold” cannot be weighed, inherited, tested, or exchanged and experience it as a trick.

The mistake becomes deliberate fraud when a broker, Lepracaun, or court agent knows the glamour will not be accepted by later human recipients.

### Cloaking Glamour

Wealthy Lepracauns and some Cluricauns use an expensive opaque glamour shell that imitates nearby textures. Four brass-buckle-like emitters sit on hat, belt, and the sheaths of the two front legs. The leg emitters are unreliable and need frequent recalibration.

## Communications

Small, usually black, egg-shaped devices support:

- **Vox:** a voice call—“vox me,” “I voxed him.”
- **Wave:** written messaging—“send a wave,” “I waved her about it.”
- **Package:** data transfer—“package me the file.”

## Specialist devices and folkloric misreadings

- **Comb Scanner:** a Banshee sonic medical scanner resembling a large comb or, to a modern observer, an electric trimmer. Its high-frequency scan is inaudible to Fey and shrill to humans. A human may activate a lost one accidentally.
- **Banshee analytic sheet:** a cloth-like membrane whose reagent fields test water for biological and chemical contamination. Red or dark results during an epidemic inspire stories of a Banshee washing the bloody cloth of those about to die.
- **Roman dodecahedron:** a hollow bronze or brass dodecahedral passive portal beacon with a different circular opening on each face and a knob at each corner. It supplies an address target; it is not itself a portal generator.
- **Rainbow Drone:** an Internal Affairs search drone used to locate hidden treasure or objects. Its multicoloured found-target display inspires the treasure at the end of a rainbow.
- **Alp-luachra / Joint-eater / Just-halver:** a discontinued experimental stomach microportal that diverted part of a human’s food to produce nutrients for Takelings and clones. Medical concerns, recipients’ disgust, and the discovery that massive salt intake could stop the device ended the programme.
- **Pulse Lantern:** a spherical double-searchlight device that releases a directed electromagnetic pulse. At a distance, its wielder may seem to carry a severed head.
- **Shoulder Helmet:** a roughly half-metre-wide black hoverbike helmet with augmented-reality vision and widely spaced cameras for depth perception. In poor visibility the rider can resemble a headless human. The front opens to reveal the face.
