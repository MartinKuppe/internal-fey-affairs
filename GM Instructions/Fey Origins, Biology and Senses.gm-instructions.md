# Fey Origins, Biology and Senses

This is an authoritative GM reference, not player knowledge. Use it to keep descriptions consistent, but reveal its facts only when the fiction earns them. In ordinary narration and metadata call the people **Fey**, **Fair Folk**, or another appropriate human term. Reserve **Elgafar** (one individual) and **Elgafari** (plural) for rare technical or deep-lore contexts.

## Elgafar

The Fey come from Elgafar, the fifth planet of Phi Virginis. Water covers about one fifth of the planet. Its ecosystem is rich and sometimes lush, but fewer oceans mean substantially less rain and fewer bodies of flowing water than on Earth. Iron is naturally rare.

### Late hidden lore: the elevation

Like many spacefaring peoples, the Elgafari were “elevated” by an immensely powerful bio-administrator acting under the Central Galactic Bureaucracy. The administrator attempted to design an ideal society around three traits: a deeply rooted sense of beauty, a genuine desire for social connection, and an almost physical inability to lie or break a bargain. Do not reveal the bio-administrator or the Central Galactic Bureaucracy as ordinary Fey knowledge; this belongs to late hidden lore.

## Body plan

An actual Fey is roughly half a human’s height. They have large black eyes, no visible ears, and a rounded, drop-shaped head extending backward and upward. Breathing spiracles sit near its rounded tip. They have six limbs: four upward-curving, tentacular legs arranged in an X and two arms ending in small, lumpy hands. Their metabolism is carbon-based but alien. Iron is poisonous to them.

Fey can stretch their legs until they approach an adult human’s height. Apparent height is also social language: a Fey may rise to assert rank or lower themselves to concede it. Green routinely rises when exercising official authority. Some high-ranking Fey extend the display with cup-shaped heels and opaque, ground-length gowns that conceal their legs and shoes.

Fey skin is tabby and two-coloured. In poor light it can be mistaken for ragged clothing. Colour commonly signals a broad occupational group, though it is not a separate Species or rigid biological caste:

- **Beige and brown — “Workers”:** most domestic Fey, including Brownies and Hobs.
- **Blue and white — “Scientists”:** researchers, intellectuals, and medical professionals, particularly Banshees.
- **Green and brown — “Naturalists”:** nature-related professions such as biologists, zoologists, and herbalists, particularly Pookas.
- **Light grey and dark grey — “Bureaucrats”:** administrative employees, managers, and clerks.
- **Black and red — “Soldiers”:** military and law-enforcement personnel, particularly Red Caps.
- **White and gold — “Courtiers”:** queens, high officials, courtiers, artists, and musicians.

## Power-gender

Fey have no biological sex. Human observers translate their power-genders as “male” and “female,” but those terms describe social authority, not reproduction. Fey aligned principally with secular hierarchy are usually addressed as male—kings, lords, auditors, tax inspectors, and institute directors. Fey aligned principally with fashion hierarchy are usually addressed as female—queens, ladies, courtesans, fashion officers, and Grace patrons. Lesser, solitary, or independent Fey may change address with patronage, office, or self-presentation.

Takelings, Changelings, and other human-derived people have human biological sex. Actual Fey do not reproduce sexually, have no genitalia, and have no sexual or romantic drive. Their friendships can be intimate and affectionate without being sexual or romantic. Do not apply this limitation to human Takelings or clones.

## Breathing, clothing, and sustenance

Fey breathe through the spiracles at the top of the head and can breathe ordinary Earth air unaided. Specialised caps regulate toxins, humidity, pressure, aquatic conditions, and prolonged field exposure. Blue Caps wear mining respirators; Brownies wear dust-and-smoke filters; Red Caps use biohazard caps; Banshee medical filters resemble hair. A court “crown” may be an elaborate respiratory manifold whose engineering has become ceremonial.

Fey tolerate a wide range of temperatures and generally do not dress for warmth. Lower-ranking Fey may wear nothing but a breathing cap; their patterned skin can look like rags. Pookas and other field personnel may wear glamour. High-ranking and courtly Fey combine physical clothes, masks, and glamour to display rank and Fashion.

Earth food is unsafe for Fey and provides no normal nutrition. Some alcohols are limited exceptions that cause pleasure or chemical effects without feeding them. Fey consume, usually in private, a nutritional concentrate nicknamed **fairy wine**, distributed by the Mothership. Much Fey food production on Earth instead supports Takelings and human-derived clones. Eating is not normally social for actual Fey; their balls and festivities are not banquets, except when food forms part of an experiment involving humans.

Natural life expectancy is roughly 200–300 Earth years and can be extended medically. The Imperial Triad is said to be thousands of years old, sustained by medical thrones.

## Senses

Most Fey senses broadly resemble human senses, with three important exceptions.

### Vision

Fey vision uses nine types of colour receptor, spanning roughly 350–850 nanometres and producing a colour space humans cannot represent. Materials that look identical to humans may be visibly different to Fey. Their ultraviolet vision also reveals normally invisible patterns on human skin, including the lines modern science calls Blaschko’s lines. In Iron Age narration, describe what is seen rather than using that modern name.

### Hearing

Fey hear approximately 10 Hz–6 kHz. They perceive infrasound but struggle with high frequencies. A Banshee’s sonic medical scanner operates around 7–8 kHz: effectively inaudible to Fey, but a shrill, uncanny wail to humans.

### Active magnetoreception

Fey can send a strong magnetic “ping” and analyse its reflection to locate nearby organisms and objects, even through a thin door. Researchers may scan a human house before entering. Ferromagnetic material, especially iron, disrupts this sense. A horseshoe above a doorway both interferes with the scan and sits beyond the reach of a small Fey.

## Narration discipline

Keep exact wavelengths, frequencies, anatomical labels, and modern scientific analogies inside technical dialogue or earned explanation. Early narration should present sensory evidence: a too-small figure rising impossibly tall, a cap covering the place where breath should enter, a wail the singer cannot hear, or a Fey hesitating beneath an iron horseshoe.
